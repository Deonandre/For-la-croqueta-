# What to submit as your two extracts

IB requires **two extracts, each max 40 lines**, one from the literary work, one from the non-literary body of work. You bring a clean copy; you may annotate it.

## EXTRACT 1 — Literary
**Langston Hughes, "Mother to Son" (first published in *The Crisis*, December 1922; collected in *The Weary Blues*, 1926).**

**20 lines — the complete poem.** This is ideal: well under the limit, and submitting it whole means every structural claim you make (the single-word line, the circular ending, the pivot at line 14) is visible to the examiner on the page.

> **The "work" is Hughes's poetry as a body.** "Mother to Son" is your extract; "Dreams" and "Harlem" are the wider work you reference. This is correct IB practice for poetry — do not let anyone tell you that you have chosen three works.

## EXTRACT 2 — Non-literary
**Dorothea Lange, *Migrant Mother* series, Nipomo, California, March 1936.
Resettlement Administration (FSA after 1937). Library of Congress, FSA/OWI Collection.**

**Submit TWO frames on one sheet, side by side.** This is the single biggest upgrade to your oral, because it converts your analysis from description into **evidence of a choice** — and Criterion B rewards exactly that.

**Left (label it "Frame A — wider"):** the image you already have. Mother seated at the lean-to; older girl leaning on her shoulder, **looking directly into the camera**; baby asleep in her lap; **unlit lantern** at lower left; tent pole slanting right; open field behind; wedding ring visible.

**Right (label it "Frame B — final"):** the canonical tight portrait. Hand at chin; both older children turned away into her shoulders; baby in lap; background dissolved to grey.

**Caption both with Lange's own filed caption:**
> *"Destitute pea pickers in California. Mother of seven children. Age thirty-two. Nipomo, California."*

### Why two frames beats one
- You can **point** at what disappears: the field, the lantern, the child's gaze, the tent.
- "Lange moves closer" stops being a claim and becomes something the examiner can see.
- The unlit lantern in Frame A is your bridge to Hughes's "where there ain't been no light." That link is only available to you if Frame A is on the sheet.
- It protects you in the Q&A: if asked "how do you know this was constructed?", you tap the page.

### Where to get the files
Both are public domain, free, high resolution, at the Library of Congress FSA/OWI Collection: search **loc.gov** for *"Migrant Mother Nipomo Lange"*. Download the TIFFs, place side by side in one A4 landscape document, print.

## A note on your global issue wording

Your registered version:
> *The impact of social and economic hardship on individuals and their determination to overcome challenges.*

This is **a topic, not an issue** — nothing in it can be argued against, so Criterion B has nowhere to go. Sharpened:

> **The representation of economic hardship and the limits of individual resilience.**
> *(Field of inquiry: Politics, power and justice.)*

The difference: the second one contains a tension you can spend ten minutes resolving.

**If you have already submitted the original wording to your teacher, keep it** — do not create a mismatch on the paperwork. Simply say the sharpened version aloud in your introduction as your framing of it. Examiners mark what you say, not what was filed, and the two are clearly the same issue.
